# CRC Public Relations design references, 5 November 2026

Brief read: CRC is a two-principal boutique corporate PR firm in Brisbane that advises senior, experienced decision makers (CEOs, board chairs, heads of associations, schools and government bodies). The site's job is trust first, then an enquiry or a call to the 24/7 line. The archetype is the Sage with a Ruler's restraint: in the first three seconds the visitor should feel they have reached experienced counsel who does not need to oversell. Brand inputs are the logo blue (#1C74BC) and white, real photography from the 2026 shoot used sparingly and front-on only, and no tall or skinny typefaces. Lyall's feedback on round two was that everything felt huge, there were far too many photos (and far too many of him), and the AI-animated photos were unwanted. Earlier notes add: reflect who they are without overclaiming, crisis is one practice among three rather than the identity, the "15 minutes" idea needs more nuance than a slogan, and copy that sounds machine-written or a "stock standard" layout counts against a site.

Visual language queried: typographic restraint, editorial serif or measured grotesk, one photograph per screen at most, navy and white with one blue accent, hairline structure instead of cards and gradients.

Sources hit: a1 (not reachable this session: the connector needs re-authorising), Mobbin (paid plan not active), web (31 live sites screenshotted and measured at 1440px, 27 loaded), 21st (5 components).

Calibration from round one: all four concepts Lyall picked (Signal, Chambers, Ember, Gallery) and all six he passed over were sans-serif. A serif direction has not been put in front of him yet. The ones he passed over were the cinematic ones (dark red, night skyline, giant type, a single large cut-out portrait, a lime-on-black tech look).

## The ten

### 1. Brunswick Group - https://www.brunswickgroup.com/
Source: web
Why it fits: The most senior firm in the category says almost nothing above the fold: one centred two-line statement in navy on a warm paper canvas. It reads as calm authority, which is the feeling CRC is after.
Take from it: the paper canvas, navy as the ink colour, a centred typographic statement in place of a hero photo, and a mid-page "who we are" paragraph set large and centred.
Leave behind: the 72px capitals (too big for Lyall) and the global-network language.
Fonts: Petit Serif headings, Helix body. Palette: canvas #F6F6EF, headline #0B0384.

### 2. Cato Counsel (formerly Cato & Clive) - https://catocounsel.com.au/
Source: web
Why it fits: An Australian boutique that advises boards and executive teams on special situations, and its site has a paragraph, a founder page and a contact page. The confidence comes from leaving things out.
Take from it: the restraint, the letter-spaced wordmark treatment, and the willingness to let one paragraph do the work.
Leave behind: the near-empty pages. CRC needs depth for search and for the insights programme.
Fonts: New Baskerville headings, Montserrat body. Palette: black canvas, text #8D847A.

### 3. Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz - https://www.wlrk.com/
Source: web
Why it fits: The firm's home page is its name in spaced small capitals on a navy field. It is the clearest example of an advisory brand that assumes the visitor already knows why they came.
Take from it: small capitals with generous tracking for labels and the nav, and serif headlines on news items.
Leave behind: the absence of any proposition on the home page.
Fonts: Playfair Display headings, Lato body.

### 4. Kekst CNC - https://www.kekstcnc.com/
Source: web
Why it fits: A reputation and crisis consultancy that leads with a plain serif sentence about protecting reputations "when it matters most", with insight cards beside it. Crisis sits inside the offer rather than in front of it.
Take from it: a serif statement paired with a short column of current insights, and the plain, specific wording.
Leave behind: the aerial stock photography and the purple.
Fonts: Lora headings, Roboto body (Roboto is not used in 3P builds).

### 5. Teneo Australia - https://www.teneo.com/australia/
Source: web
Why it fits: "Integrated, senior-led counsel" is the promise CRC also makes ("you deal directly with an experienced adviser"). The people section uses small round portraits with name and title, which keeps faces present without filling the page with them.
Take from it: the senior-led line as a short statement under the headline, and the small round portraits.
Leave behind: the city-skyline hero and the global scale.
Fonts: Neue Haas Grotesk Display headings.

### 6. C. Hoare & Co. - https://www.hoaresbank.co.uk/
Source: web
Why it fits: A private bank whose hero is split in two: a deep coloured panel with a short capitalised statement and one photograph of a room, not a person. It feels discreet and personal, which is how CRC describes its service.
Take from it: the split-panel hero with one interior photograph, and "letters" from the principal as the editorial voice.
Leave behind: the green and the heritage ornament.
Fonts: Lato measured on page text; the hero face was not measured.

### 7. Gresham - https://gresham.au/
Source: web
Why it fits: An Australian independent advisory firm with a modest serif statement (50px, light), one strip of office photography and three plainly described businesses. Nothing is oversized and nothing is oversold.
Take from it: the headline size and weight, office photography in place of people, and three practice blocks with small capital titles and a square button.
Leave behind: the dense small body copy inside the cards.
Fonts: Caslon headings (weight 200), Montserrat body. Palette: white canvas, headline #363341.

### 8. Lazard - https://www.lazard.com/
Source: web
Why it fits: An editorial two-column introduction (a serif statement on the left, plain explanatory copy on the right) that suits a firm whose principal writes and comments in the media.
Take from it: the two-column statement-and-explanation pattern for the "why a boutique firm" section.
Leave behind: the news-headline hero and the 96px type.
Fonts: VC Garamond headings (light). Palette: white canvas, headline #23283C.

### 9. Centerview Partners - https://www.centerviewpartners.com/
Source: web
Why it fits: An independent advisory firm that states its record as a quiet row of light numerals divided by hairlines on a pale band. It shows proof without shouting it.
Take from it: the stat row (light numerals, hairline dividers, short labels) on a pale blue band.
Leave behind: the full-bleed interior hero photograph.
Fonts: Roboto Slab headings (light), system body.

### 10. Gilbert + Tobin - https://www.gtlaw.com.au/
Source: web
Why it fits: An Australian firm in navy with fine blue linework standing in for photography, and a sector list set as quiet chips. It is the closest of the ten to CRC's own colours.
Take from it: navy and logo blue, vertical linework as the hero graphic, and the sector chip grid.
Leave behind: the single cut-out portrait in the hero (the round-one concept with a large cut-out portrait was passed over).
Fonts: Founders Grotesque throughout. Palette: headline #060D32.

## Recommended direction

Build three new directions, each as the same four pages as the shortlisted four, so Lyall and Barbara can compare like for like.

- Alt 13 Letterhead (Brunswick, Cato Counsel, Wachtell, Kekst): paper canvas, navy ink, centred serif statements at a modest size, small tracked capitals for labels, hairline rules instead of cards. The hero has no photograph; one photo of the two of them sits mid-page. This is the first serif option CRC will have seen.
- Alt 14 Private Office (C. Hoare & Co., Gresham, Lazard): a split hero with a deep navy panel and one photograph of the office, light tracked capitals in a sturdy grotesk, square buttons, and plain bordered practice blocks. The people section is text only, so the pair appear in one photograph on the whole home page.
- Alt 15 Linework (Gilbert + Tobin, Teneo, Centerview): navy and logo blue, blue linework in place of a hero photograph, a senior-led statement, a sector chip grid, a pale stat row and small round portraits.

The choice for Alex: whether to put all three in front of Lyall, or lead with Letterhead and Private Office as the two that move furthest from what he has already seen.

## Not used and why

- Joele Frank: a 195px headline, exactly the "everything is huge" problem.
- Instinctif and Lansons: condensed display faces (Bebas, Anton) are tall and skinny, which the client ruled out.
- FGS Global, Moelis, Evercore and Centerview's own hero: text laid over large stock or interior photography, which reads as the "stock standard" look.
- Egon Zehnder: a consultant grid of large photographs, too many faces for this client.
- Powerscourt, Hudson Sandler, Tulchan and Perella Weinberg: blocked the headless browser, so they could not be measured.
